'Item 1A. Risk Factors
- -------- ------------

 The following are certain risk factors that could affect Zoltek\'s\nbusiness, financial results and results of operations. These risk factors
 should be considered in connection with evaluating the forward-looking
 statements contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K because these
 factors could cause the actual results and conditions to differ materially
 from those projected in forward-looking statements. Before you buy the\nCompany\'s common stock, you should know that making such an investment
 involves some risks, including the risks described below. The risks that the\nCompany has highlighted here are not the only ones that the Company faces.
 If any of the risks actually occur, the Company\'s business, financial\ncondition or results of operations could be negatively affected. In that
 case, the trading price of its stock could decline, and you may lose all or\npart of your investment.
 
 ZOLTEK HAS REPORTED NET LOSSES AND NEGATIVE CASH FLOWS FROM
 OPERATIONS FOR EACH OF THE PAST FIVE FISCAL YEARS.
 
 Zoltek has reported net losses and negative cash flows from
 continuing operations for each of the past five fiscal years in the period
 ended September 30, 2005. The Company reported net losses of 31.6 million,
 7.8 million, 15.6 million, 22.8 million and 40.4 million in the fiscal
 years ended September 30, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004 and 2005, respectively. The
 Company reported negative cash flows from continuing operations of 6.2
 million, 2.0 million, 2.2 million, 7.7 million and 7.4 million in the
 fiscal years ended September 30, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004 and 2005,
 respectively. These net losses and negative cash flows from operations were
 attributable to, among other things, the adverse market conditions discussed
 above, combined with excess capacities and inventories it has maintained in
 anticipation of greater sales volumes and in 2004 and 2005 the non-cash loss
 related to the mark to market of derivatives of 4.9 million and 16.6
 million, respectively. The Company cannot assure you when, if at all, it
 will return to profitable operations or generate cash flows from operations.
 The Company has relied on equity financing and borrowings to finance its
 business over the past five fiscal years. The Company intends to fund its\
 near-term continuing operations from cost-containment, aggressive management
 of its working capital and additional bank borrowings, private equity and
 debt financing. Such additional funding may not be available on favorable
 terms or at all. If adequate funds are not otherwise available, the Company
 may be forced to curtail operations and/or development activities
 significantly, or seek other sources of capital, including asset sales.
 
  ZOLTEK\'S OPERATING RESULTS HAVE BEEN ADVERSELY AFFECTED BY
  PREVIOUSLY ADVERSE CONDITIONS IN THE MARKETS FOR CARBON FIBER AND ACRYLIC
  FIBER.
  
   Until fiscal 2004, Zoltek\'s carbon fiber operations were adversely\naffected during the preceding five years by oversupply conditions in the\nmarketplace, coupled with an overall lack of development for large volume\napplications for carbon fiber composites. Accordingly, during such period\nthe Company had excess carbon fiber manufacturing capacity which resulted in\nsubstantial depreciation and other charges and constrained the operating\nresults of its carbon fiber manufacturing activities. Since the beginning of\nfiscal 2004, the market has strengthened for current and emerging\napplications for carbon fiber products, and it has generated meaningful\norders and experienced significant sales increases in carbon fiber products.\nHowever, the Company may not be able to attain anticipated sales increases\nunless the market and demand for current and emerging applications for\ncarbon fiber products continues to strengthen.\n\n Since fiscal 1998, the Company\'s results have been negatively\nimpacted by depressed market conditions in the acrylic fibers sector, which\nwas a principal line of business for its Zoltek Rt. subsidiary. In the\nfourth quarter of fiscal 2004, the Company discontinued and exited two\ndivisions of its Zoltek Rt. operations which manufacture acrylic and nylon\nfibers and yarns. In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2004, the Company recorded\nan impairment loss on discontinued operations of 0.2 million related to\nseverance obligations due to discontinuance of these businesses. In the\nfourth quarter of fiscal 2005, the Company formally adopted a plan to\ndiscontinue the CMC division of its Zoltek Rt. operations and does not\nexpect any impairment loss related to discontinuing of this operation.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S OPERATING RESULTS HAVE BEEN ADVERSELY AFFECTED BY\nDIFFICULTIES IN OPERATING THE COMPANY\'S EXPANDED CARBON FIBERS CAPACITY AT\nITS ABILENE, TEXAS FACILITY.\n\n Demand for Zoltek\'s carbon fibers from existing and potential new\ncustomers exceeds its current capacity, and the Company has restarted carbon\nfiber production at its major carbon fiber manufacturing facility in\nAbilene, Texas which had been temporarily idled. However, the Company\npresently cannot produce carbon fibers at levels adequate to supply current\ndemand. The Company has experienced difficulties in achieving targeted\nproduction levels at its Abilene facility, which has resulted in operating\nlosses despite strong demand for its products. The difficulties were due in\nlarge part the inability to recruit and train qualified workers and managers\nat the plant that had been dormant for several years. The Company may not be\nable to supply anticipated demand unless it is able to achieve targeted\nproduction levels at its Abilene facility.\n\n 11\n\nZOLTEK\'S ABILITY TO MANAGE ITS ANTICIPATED GROWTH WILL AFFECT ITS\nOPERATING RESULTS.\n\n The growth in the Company\'s business has placed, and is expected to\ncontinue to place, a significant strain on its management and operations. In\norder to effectively manage potential long-term growth, the Company must add\nto its carbon fiber manufacturing capacity, have access to adequate\nfinancial resources to fund significant capital expenditures and maintain\ngross profit margins while pursuing a growth strategy, continuing to\nstrengthen its operations, financial and management information systems, and\nexpanding, training and managing its employee workforce. There can be no\nassurance that the Company will be able to do so effectively or on a timely\nbasis. Failure to do so effectively and on a timely basis could have a\nmaterial adverse effect upon its business, operating results and financial\ncondition.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S OPERATIONS AND SALES IN FOREIGN COUNTRIES ARE SUBJECT TO\nRISKS.\n\n Zoltek\'s international operations and sales are subject to risks\nassociated with foreign operations and markets generally, including foreign\ncurrency fluctuations, unexpected changes in regulatory, economic or\npolitical conditions, tariffs and other trade barriers, longer accounts\nreceivable payment cycles, potentially adverse tax consequences,\nrestrictions on repatriation of earnings and the burdens of complying with a\nwide variety of foreign laws. These factors could have a material adverse\neffect upon the Company\'s future revenues and business, results of\noperations and financial condition.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S OPERATIONS ARE DEPENDENT UPON ITS SENIOR MANAGEMENT AND\nTECHNICAL PERSONNEL.\n\n Zoltek\'s future operating results depend upon the continued service\nof its senior management, including Zsolt Rumy, the Company\'s Chief\nExecutive Officer, President and Chairman of the Board, and its technical\npersonnel, none of whom are bound by an employment agreement. See "Item 9A. Controls and Procedures." A material\nweakness is defined by the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United\nStates) as a control deficiency, or combination of control deficiencies,\nthat results in more than a remote likelihood that a material misstatement\nof the annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or\ndetected. A control deficiency exists when the design or operation of a\ncontrol does not allow management or employees, in the normal course of\nperforming their assigned functions, to prevent or detect misstatements on a\ntimely basis. As set forth in Item 9A. Controls and Procedures, as of\nSeptember 30, 2005, the Company identified material weaknesses in its\ngeneral control environment and its controls over period-end financial\nreporting process, journal entries and account reconciliations,\ndetermination of allowance for doubtful accounts, accounting for inventory,\nvaluation of property plant and equipment, accounting for derivatives\nassociated with convertible debt and related amortization of financing fees\nand debt discount and gain (loss) on value of warrants and conversion\nfeature, earnings per share disclosures, segregation of duties and\nrestricted access. These control deficiencies resulted in accounting errors\nwhich required the restatement of its previously issued financial statements\ncontained in its Annual Report on Form 10-K for the year ended September 30,\n2004, and its Quarterly Reports on Form 10-Q for the quarters ended March\n31, 2004, June 30, 2004, December 31, 2004 and March 31, 2005. The Company\nis currently evaluating steps that can be taken to remediate these\nweaknesses in its internal controls, including steps that can be taken in\nthe process of documenting and evaluating the applicable treatment for\nnon-routine or complex transactions as they may arise.\n\n Zoltek expects to incur significant expenses associated with these\ncorrective actions to remedy the identified material weaknesses. If the\nremedial policies and procedures the Company implements are insufficient to\naddress the material weaknesses that exist as of September 30, 2005, or if\nadditional material weaknesses in its internal controls are discovered in\nthe future, the Company may fail to meet its future reporting obligations,\nits financial statements may contain material misstatements and its\noperating results may be harmed. Any such failure could also adversely\naffect the results of the periodic management evaluations and annual auditor\nattestation reports regarding the effectiveness of the Company\'s "internal\ncontrol over financial reporting" that are required by the SEC\'s rules under\nSection 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002. The existence of a material\nweakness could result in errors in the Company\'s financial statements that\ncould result in a restatement of financial statements, cause it to fail to\nmeet its reporting obligations and in turn cause investors to lose\nconfidence in its reported financial information, leading to a decline in\nthe Company\'s stock price. Although the Company believes it can address its\nmaterial weaknesses in internal controls with remedial measures, it cannot\nassure you that the measures it will take will remediate the material\nweaknesses identified or that any additional material weaknesses will not\narise in the future due to a failure to implement and maintain adequate\ninternal controls over financial reporting.\n\n DEVELOPMENT OF NEW MARKETS FOR ZOLTEK CARBON FIBER PRODUCTS IS\nUNCERTAIN.\n\n Zoltek\'s future profitability and growth will depend in part upon\nthe development of new markets for a broad range of large-scale applications\nwhich incorporate the Company\'s carbon fiber products. The Company\nbelieves that its current plant design and operating procedures minimize\noperational risks associated with these factors. However, as a result of\nmechanical or human failure or unforeseen conditions or events related to\nits manufacturing and engineering processes or otherwise, its manufacturing\ncapacity could be materially limited or temporarily interrupted.\n\n ONGOING LITIGATION IN WHICH ZOLTEK IS INVOLVED COULD RESULT IN ITS\nHAVING TO PAY SUBSTANTIAL DAMAGES.\n\n Zoltek is party to various legal actions as either plaintiff or\ndefendant. As previously disclosed, the Company is party to an action filed\nin the Court of Common Pleas of Cuyahoga County, Ohio, by the former owners\nof Hardcore Composites Operations LLC against each of Hardcore and the\nCompany alleging breach by Hardcore and the Company of its respective\nobligations under a sublease, guaranty and settlement agreement entered into\nin connection with the former owner\'s sale of Hardcore. The Company has recorded an accrual of 1.1 million\nin respect of the possible liability in this matter. The Company is\nvigorously defending this motion and it has filed counterclaims and an\nappeal. Additionally, as previously disclosed in September 2004 the Company\nwas named a defendant in a civil action filed by a former investment banker\nthat was retained by it to obtain equity investors, alleging that it\nbreached its obligations under the agreement signed by the parties. The\ninvestment banker alleges that it is owed commission from equity investment\nthe Company obtained from a different source. The ultimate outcome of these\nactions and other pending litigation and the estimates of the potential\nfuture impact on its financial position, cash flows or results of operations\nfor these proceedings could have a material adverse effect on its business.\nIn addition, the Company may incur additional legal costs in connection with\npursuing and defending such actions.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S PRINCIPAL SHAREHOLDER HAS VOTING CONTROL OVER THE COMPANY.\n\n Zsolt Rumy, Zoltek\'s founder and principal shareholder, owns\napproximately 30 of the Company\'s outstanding shares of common stock. As a\nresult, he has effective voting control over the Company, including the\nelection of directors, and is able to effectively prevent an affirmative\nvote which would be necessary for a merger, sale of assets or similar\ntransaction, irrespective of whether other shareholders believe such a\ntransaction to be in their best interests. The Company\'s Articles of\nIncorporation and By-laws do not provide for cumulative voting in the\nelection of directors.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S BOARD OF DIRECTORS HAS THE AUTHORITY TO ISSUE PREFERRED\nSTOCK WHICH COULD AFFECT THE RIGHTS OF HOLDERS OF COMMON STOCK.\n\n Zoltek\'s Articles of Incorporation authorize the issuance of "blank\ncheck" preferred stock with such designations, rights and preferences as may\nbe determined from time to time by the Board of Directors. Accordingly, the\nBoard of Directors is empowered, without shareholder approval, to issue\npreferred stock with dividend, liquidation, conversion, voting or other\nrights which could adversely affect the voting power or other rights of the\nholders of common stock. Holders of common stock will have no preemptive\nrights to subscribe for a pro rata portion of any preferred stock which may\nbe issued. In the event of issuance, the preferred stock could be utilized,\nunder certain circumstances, as a method of discouraging, delaying or\npreventing a change in control. The possible impact on takeover could\nadversely affect the price of the common stock. Although the Company has no\npresent intention to issue any shares of preferred stock, it may do so in\nthe future.\n\n ZOLTEK\'S CLASSIFIED BOARD OF DIRECTORS COULD DISCOURAGE A CHANGE IN\nCONTROL.\n\n Zoltek\'s Articles of Incorporation divide the Board of Directors\ninto three classes, with three-year staggered terms. The classified board\nprovision could increase the likelihood that, in the event an outside party\nacquired a controlling block of the Company\'s stock, incumbent directors\nnevertheless would retain their positions for a substantial period, which\nmay have the effect of discouraging, delaying or preventing a change in\ncontrol. The possible impact on takeover attempts could adversely affect the\nprice of the common stock.\n\n 15\n\nFUTURE SALES OF COMMON STOCK COULD AFFECT THE PRICE OF COMMON\nSTOCK.\n\n No prediction can be made as to the effect, if any, that future\nsales of shares or the availability of shares for sale will have on the\nmarket price of the common stock prevailing from time to time.
